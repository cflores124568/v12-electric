# V12 Electric city hero prompts

Nine replacements for `public/images/cities/*-hero.webp`. Written as photo briefs, not filled-in forms — a lens, an hour, a sun angle, a color temperature, and a subject. Paste one block per generation.

The current nine are a single recipe repeated: cobalt blue hour, silhouetted ridge, 2700K amber uplighting, tan stucco, palms. You cannot grade your way out of that, because the sameness is the *hour*. Every brief below sets a different time of day, so the palette separates on its own and stays physically true.

**Every image**: 1672 × 941, 16:9. The headline overlay eats the left 52% of the frame, so the left third stays quiet and mid-value and all subject weight sits right of center. Real electrical practice — closed enclosures, strapped conduit, sane mounting heights, nobody working live, no sparks. No text, no signage, no logos, no watermark. Photographic, full-frame, natural optics. Not a render.

## Palm Springs

> Shot on a 35mm at f/5.6, late afternoon, and the whole town is already in shadow — Mount San Jacinto's east face is a near-vertical 10,000-foot wall a mile west and it kills the sun early while the sky overhead is still blazing. That inversion is the picture: cool blue-grey open shade on the ground, hot bleached sky above. A restored 1960s Alexander sits in the right 45% of the frame in three-quarter view, flat roofline with a deep overhang on a slim steel post, breeze-block screen wall, clerestory band, white gravel roof, butterfly carport. The mountain fills the entire background at close range, grey granite, every ravine resolved, running out the top of the frame rather than sitting politely on a horizon. Left third is bare: pale concrete drive, white gravel, one clean shadow. A slim Level 2 wall charger is mounted on the carport wall with a short square conduit drop, cable coiled, a grey sedan parked beneath it unplugged, and low-voltage grazing lights just barely reading on the breeze-block. Cool shadow, warm sky, mint and coral on the door and planters. Kodak Portra, not real-estate HDR.
>
> No amber landscape glow. No dusk. No blue hour. The mountain is lit rock, never a silhouette.

## Cathedral City

> 50mm at f/8, straight-on, hard midday sun directly overhead — the unglamorous hour nobody shoots, which is exactly why it belongs here. An ordinary 1980s single-story tract house, beige stucco, brown trim, concrete tile roof, roll-up garage door open, tired oleander hedge, chain-link side gate. House and garage in the right 45%; left third is empty asphalt street and a strip of dead parkway grass. Flat valley floor behind, one hazy distant ridge, no mountain drama. A plain white unmarked service van at the curb in the middle distance. On the garage wall the exterior meter main and subpanel are open, deadfront cover lifted off and set flat against the wall, organized copper and breakers visible, a clamp meter and torque screwdriver resting on a folded drop cloth — diagnosis in progress, nobody's hands on anything. Sky bleached almost white, shadows short and hard and black under the eave, everything dusty and desaturated. Documentary, mildly unflattering, completely believable.
>
> No golden hour, no dusk, no night. No glamour landscaping. Van stays blank white.

## Desert Hot Springs

> 24mm at f/11, an hour after sunrise, wind moving hard from the west. This city sits on an exposed bench above the valley floor and it looks nothing like the resort towns — creosote scrub instead of turf, no palms, decomposed granite yards, and the San Gorgonio Pass wind farm standing on the horizon. Compose wide and low: horizon in the bottom 40%, sky taking the rest, a modest flat-roofed white stucco block house small in the right 40% and genuinely dwarfed by the space around it. Left two thirds is open creosote and sand running to a haze of white turbines, softened by dust and atmospheric perspective until they're barely more than pale verticals. On the side wall, a new service panel and disconnect with dead-straight rigid conduit climbing to the weatherhead, plus a small ground-mounted equipment pad — recent work, tidy, inspected. Pale white-gold light raking in from the left, sage grey-green scrub, bone sand, washed pale sky. Desaturated, airy, huge.
>
> No palms. No lawn. No pool. Turbines stay distant and hazy — background, not graphic.

## Rancho Mirage

> 85mm at f/2.8, golden hour with the sun still genuinely up. This is the green city — clipped ficus walls, mature olives, citrus in pots, turf that gets watered. Look down a private drive with the wall and hedge pulling the eye right toward a single-story desert-modern entry in the right 40%: limestone cladding, deep loggia, pivot door, low seat wall. Left third is lawn with one long raking shadow thrown by an olive tree, and nothing else. Santa Rosa mountains warm and fully lit behind, never silhouetted. The electrical story is control, not power: a discreet keypad beside the entry and a lighting scene just coming up while sun is still on the facade — soft downlights in the loggia soffit, low grazing on the limestone, present but clearly subordinate to daylight. Low sun raking from the left, deep saturated lawn, cream limestone, silver-green olive, long soft shadows, warm blue upper sky. Lush and expensive without trying.
>
> Sun is up. Not dusk, not blue hour. No EV charger. No visible panel.

## Palm Desert

> 35mm at f/4, soft overcast, shallow-ish depth. The valley's commercial street — an upscale El Paseo-style storefront under a flowering jacaranda canopy throwing dappled purple-blue shade across the sidewalk. Streetside three-quarter view, storefront in the right 45%, left third just empty shaded sidewalk and a jacaranda trunk, distant shopfronts blurred out. Clean contemporary frontage: big glass, thin dark mullions, plaster and board-formed concrete, recessed entry, planted verge. A small tidy junction box and photocell on the fascia. Flat neutral overcast outside, silver-grey concrete, purple-blue bloom, and 3000K interior light as the only warm thing in the frame. Cool shell, warm core.
>

## Indian Wells

> 50mm at f/8, sunrise, absolutely still. The quietest city in the valley: enormous setbacks, ranked date palms, immaculate turf, low walls, nobody out. Compose long and horizontal down a wide boulevard — a low wall and a rank of evenly spaced mature date palms receding through the left two thirds in steady rhythm, residence entry court in the right 35%. Low-slung contemporary with wide horizontal stone banding, deep flat overhang, glass entry, gravel-and-turf verge, zero clutter. Santa Rosas behind catching first pink alpenglow. Electrically it's almost nothing on purpose: a flush exterior equipment enclosure, low bollard path lights still faintly on from overnight, a slim camera and network plate near the entry, everything aligned to the millimeter. Rose-gold sky, pink on the rock, cool blue residual shadow on the ground, wet green turf still holding dew. Cold-to-warm gradient straight across the frame.
>
> Sunrise with pink on the mountains — not sunset, not blue hour. No cars. No people. No pool.

## La Quinta

> 35mm at f/8, mid-morning, sun high and hard from the left. The Cove's defining fact is proximity: the Santa Rosa Mountains stand a few hundred feet behind the back wall, red-brown, fully lit, every ridge and ravine and rock face resolved — the opposite of a distant blue silhouette. Spanish colonial revival home in the right 45%, three-quarter on the front courtyard and garage: white lime plaster, terracotta barrel tile, dark stained wood door with iron hardware, arched courtyard opening, magenta bougainvillea spilling over the wall, saltillo step. Left third is a plain chalk-white plaster wall in shade carrying one clean diagonal shadow. A Level 2 charger on the garage wall, short conduit drop to a subpanel, cable holstered, modern SUV on the paver drive — the install squared to the plaster joint lines. Hot white plaster, orange tile, red rock, magenta bloom, deep blue-green sky. The most saturated frame in the set.
>
> Mountain is lit and detailed, never a silhouette. No dusk, no night, no amber landscape lighting.

## Indio

> 28mm at f/5.6, full sunset, dust in the air. Date country — ordered rows of tall shaggy palms, agricultural grid, dead-flat land, heat still coming off the ground at the end of the day. The sky carries this one: grove rows run diagonally through the left two thirds as dark repetitive trunks against a burning sky, a plain single-story ranch and its driveway in the right 35%. Stucco, tile roof, deep carport, gravel and dirt yard, farm gate, everything practical and nothing decorative. Horizon low and flat, no mountain wall anywhere. On the wall, an exterior panel with a fresh dedicated circuit and weatherproof outlet feeding an irrigation controller, conduit run clean along the stucco, and a single wall pack just flickering on as the light goes. Orange to magenta to violet across the sky, green-black frond silhouettes, ground already dropping into shadow. Saturated warm sky, cool dark earth.
>
> True sunset with color in the sky — not blue hour, not night. No mountains. No EV charger.

## Coachella

> 35mm at f/4, deep blue hour. The only twilight frame in the set, so it has to be unmistakably agricultural and commercial or it collapses back into the old look. Shoot across a table-grape block toward a corrugated metal packing shed in the right 40% — trellis rows and drip line receding low and orderly and dark through the left two thirds, the pale dry Mecca Hills sitting low behind and holding the last light. Concrete apron, steel canopy, loading dock, stacked field bins, no ornament at all. Roll-up door open with the interior lit. On the shed exterior, a three-phase service — meter, disconnect, rigid conduit neatly strapped and rising to the roofline — and hard cool 4000K high-bay light spilling out the open door across the concrete. Deep blue sky, cool industrial white, dark vine green, dusty concrete, pale hills. Cool on cool with one brutal light source.
>
> Commercial and agricultural, never a home. No palms. No cars. No warm amber anything.

## After you generate

1. Export 1672 × 941, convert to WebP, drop into `public/images/cities/`. Filenames are referenced from `src/data/service-areas.ts` — don't rename them.
2. Rewrite every `heroAlt` in `src/data/service-areas.ts`. They all describe blue-hour scenes and six of these are now daylight.
3. `src/styles/global.css:5490-5498` hardcodes the hero scrim as `rgb(3 10 19)`. That navy will drag all six daylight images straight back toward cobalt. Tokenize it per city or this whole exercise gets undone by three gradient rules.
